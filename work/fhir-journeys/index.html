The problem

Patient-journey analytics starts with messy interoperability data, and one broken reference can quietly skew every count downstream. I generated a synthetic FHIR R4 bundle, validated it so every reference resolves, then mined treatment patterns, prototyped a therapy recommender and mapped the referral network between providers.

How it works

  1. Generate

    A seeded generator builds 500 synthetic patients and 8,478 FHIR R4 resources, with comorbidities, therapy lines and referral chains.

  2. Validate

    The parser checks required fields and confirms every reference resolves before any analysis runs.

  3. Mine

    Medication sequences and co-prescription pairs, scored by support and lift.

  4. Recommend

    A cohort-similarity recommender weights condition overlap 0.70, age 0.15 and sex 0.15, and never suggests a drug the patient already takes.

  5. Map

    A networkx referral graph of HCPs and HCOs with centrality, hubs and communities.

How I measured it

  • 8,478 FHIR R4 resources for 500 synthetic patients, with every reference resolved
  • Top co-prescription lift 22.78 on 18 patients (support 0.044), counting only pairs seen in at least 5 patients
  • Referral network: 39 nodes, 211 edges, 481 referrals and 3 communities

Limits

  • Synthetic patients only. This is a decision-support prototype, not medical advice, and it is not clinically validated.
  • Patterns like the chemotherapy doublet were designed into the generator, so finding them checks the code rather than discovering something new.
  • The recommender is unit-tested for its ranking and never suggests a drug the patient already takes, but it has no holdout evaluation.

What I'd tell the team

  • Reuse the validation step first: count nothing until every reference in the bundle resolves.
  • Treat the recommender as a prototype until it is scored on held-out patients.
