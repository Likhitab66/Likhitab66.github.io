The problem

In health economics, an AI that invents a cost-effectiveness figure is worse than no AI at all. I built a retrieval pipeline over 40 synthetic HEOR documents about fictional drugs, plus a checker that flags any answer sentence whose numbers do not appear in the retrieved evidence.

How it works

  1. Corpus

    40 synthetic HEOR documents across 8 therapy areas and 5 document types, each with fictional drugs and results.

  2. Index

    Documents are split into 120-word windows with a 30-word overlap and indexed in ChromaDB with its default MiniLM embeddings, plus a TF-IDF retriever as a fallback.

  3. Retrieve

    The top chunks go into a prompt with inline source tags, so every sentence can point to where it came from.

  4. Generate

    A local Ollama backend, or an echo demo mode that returns the retrieved evidence verbatim so the pipeline runs without a model.

  5. Check

    The grounding checker scores word overlap per sentence and requires every number in the answer to appear in the evidence.

  6. Evaluate

    25 questions, each tied to one known source document, scored with hit-rate at k and MRR.

How I measured it

  • On two hand-built examples, the checker scored the grounded answer 0.899 and the hallucinated one 0.322, and flagged its invented figure
  • Gold reference answers scored 0.977 mean supportability against the retrieved evidence
  • Hit-rate@1, @3 and @5 and MRR all 1.00 for both retrievers, on an easy benchmark

Limits

  • The benchmark is easy: each question names a unique fictional drug, so perfect retrieval says more about the test than the retriever.
  • Results come from retrieval and demo mode. No LLM-generated answers are stored or scored.
  • All documents, drugs and results are synthetic.

What I'd tell the team

  • Use the number check as a gate before an answer reaches a reader: any figure the retrieved evidence does not contain gets flagged.
  • Before trusting it on real documents, score it on model-written answers and on a harder benchmark where questions do not name the drug.
